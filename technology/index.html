SolarEX Technology — Mechanism-Led PV Glass Coating Selection

SolarEX is built around two distinct nanocoating pathways for photovoltaic glass: passive SiO₂ surface repellence and active TiO₂ photocatalysis. Technology selection is based on UV availability, contamination profile, operating environment and O&M objective.

Engineering principle

Surface coating selection is not a commodity choice.

The mechanism by which a coating modifies PV glass behavior must match the actual contamination profile, UV availability and O&M logic of the site.

Optical interface protection

The glass-air interface is where soiling impacts irradiance transmission. SolarEX modifies this interface at nano-scale while preserving optical clarity.

Surface energy control

Quartz uses SiO₂ to reduce contaminant adhesion through hydrophobic and oleophobic surface behavior.

Photocatalytic chemistry

Titan uses TiO₂ to generate UV-supported reactive oxygen species and decompose organic contamination.

UV dependency

UV availability is the primary pathway divider.

Quartz and Titan differ most sharply in their relationship to sunlight. Quartz remains passive and UV-independent. Titan requires UV photons to activate TiO₂ photocatalysis.

Quartz — UV-independent

Quartz is selected when the site requires passive, always-available surface repellence. It is suited for high-latitude, diffuse-light, seasonal and low-UV contexts where active photocatalysis would be less reliable.

  • No UV activation required.
  • Passive hydrophobic / oleophobic response.
  • Preferred when inorganic soiling dominates.

Titan — UV-activated

Titan is selected when sunlight provides enough UV activation and the contamination profile justifies active decomposition rather than passive repellence alone.

  • UV photons activate TiO₂.
  • Reactive oxygen species decompose organic residues.
  • Hydrophilic rinse behavior develops under activation.
Selection workflow

From site conditions to route recommendation.

1

Define site and climate context

Location, irradiance, UV availability, rainfall pattern, dust exposure, industrial surroundings and cleaning access define the operating envelope.

2

Identify dominant contamination profile

Classify soiling as inorganic/mineral, organic/biological, industrial atmospheric or mixed. This determines whether passive repellence or active decomposition is technically preferred.

3

Match mechanism to objective

Choose Quartz for passive adhesion reduction; choose Titan for UV-supported organic decomposition and hydrophilic rinse response.

4

Define pilot and monitoring design

Use treated/control areas, baseline production data, cleaning logs, UV/irradiance context and interval monitoring before scale-up.

Application dependency

Technology performance depends on controlled application.

A nanocoating pathway is not only chemistry. Surface preparation, residue removal, coverage, temperature, cure profile and monitoring design are part of the technical system.

Surface cleanliness

Both routes require clean, dry glass. Grease, silicone residues, surfactants, tensides and atmospheric residues can compromise bonding and test validity.

Coverage discipline

Quartz and Titan use different coverage rates and methods. Over-application or uncontrolled wet application can reduce consistency.

Cure and activation

Quartz needs cure handling. Titan requires cure handling plus sunlight exposure for the full photocatalytic effect to develop.
