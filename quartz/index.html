SolarEX Quartz — Passive Surface Engineering for PV Glass

Mechanism

The passive pathway for soiling adhesion control.

Quartz is engineered for environments where passive surface repellence is the technically appropriate solution. It requires no UV activation, no photocatalytic trigger and no on-site energy input to function.

No UV activation required

Quartz delivers passive surface repellence from point of cure. UV irradiance is not a prerequisite, making it viable at any latitude and in diffuse-light environments.

Hydrophobic response

Water beads on the coated surface and is more likely to roll off, helping remove loosely held particulates under rain or cleaning action.

Oleophobic response

The surface reduces adhesion of oily grime and environmental residues that contribute to optical-interface contamination.

Easy-clean logic

Quartz does not remove the need for O&M. It reduces adhesion and supports faster, less abrasive cleaning cycles.

Application protocol

Quartz application workflow from source instructions.

Quartz performance depends on surface preparation, residue removal, coverage control, temperature discipline and cure handling.

1

Clean and decontaminate

Clean the PV glass thoroughly. Remove heavy contaminants, insect remains, traffic film, grease, silicone residues, surfactants and tensides. Basic wipe-down is not sufficient for reliable bonding.

2

Rinse and dry

Rinse with clean water where required and dry fully. The glass surface must be clean, dry and residue-free before coating.

3

Final wipe and controlled application

Perform a final alcohol-compatible wipe. Apply by HVLP spray or pre-impregnated wipe/lint-free cloth according to the selected process.

4

Cure and protect

Allow the coating to cure. Full cure is approximately 24 hours; source framing states rain tolerance after approximately 1 hour under relevant conditions.

Operating fit

Where Quartz creates the strongest operational fit.

Dust and mineral soiling

Best fit for inorganic and mineral particulate deposition, including dust, pollen, salt and calcium/mineral residue profiles.

High-latitude / low-UV sites

Quartz is preferred where UV-dependent photocatalytic activation is not reliable enough for the operating profile.

Rainfall-assisted contexts

Reduced adhesion helps rainfall or cleaning water carry away loosely held particles more effectively than untreated glass.

Water-scarce O&M

Quartz can support extended cleaning intervals and reduced O&M burden where water access and cleaning mobilization are commercial constraints.

Evidence and ROI

Quartz evidence is used with defined context.

Europe ROI scenario

Quartz source material uses model assumptions such as 2,335 sunshine hours/year, €0.289/kWh tariff, coating-cost assumptions and a 10% performance scenario to derive an indicative approximately 147-day payback framework.

Scandinavian controlled test

A Scandinavian controlled context recorded approximately 10% output advantage for treated modules versus untreated comparators. The result supports high-latitude Quartz positioning.

Middle East cleanliness reference

High-dust and water-scarce operating conditions support the easy-clean value proposition. Field observations show visibly improved post-rain cleanliness on treated glass in source context.

Pilot requirements

How to validate Quartz before scale-up.

Baseline yield

Collect pre-coating production data for treated and control areas, ideally normalized by irradiance.

Matched control

Use modules or strings with comparable age, tilt, orientation, module type and exposure profile.

Cleaning log

Track cleaning frequency, method, water volume, labor input and visible soiling conditions.

Review period

Use at least a practical seasonal window; longer monitoring improves representativeness for procurement decisions.
