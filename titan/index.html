Active chemistry

The active pathway for organic and atmospheric fouling.

Titan is engineered for sites where UV availability is sufficient and contamination profiles include organic matter, biological fouling or complex industrial atmospheric loads.

UV-triggered ROS generation

TiO₂ nanoparticles absorb UV photons and generate reactive oxygen species at the surface interface, initiating contaminant breakdown at molecular level.

Organic contaminant decomposition

Photocatalytic activity targets organic films, VOC-like residues and biological loads rather than only passively shedding them.

Hydrophilic rinse behavior

Under UV exposure, the TiO₂ surface develops hydrophilic or superhydrophilic behavior, enabling water to sheet uniformly across glass.

Mechanism

The TiO₂ mechanism.

Titan’s active chemistry is built around titanium dioxide nanoparticles deposited on the solar glass substrate. UV photons generate electron-hole pairs and reactive oxygen species, including hydroxyl radicals, at the surface interface.

1

TiO₂ nanoparticles on glass

Uniform deposition creates the active photocatalytic surface layer.

2

UV photon absorption

UV exposure generates electron-hole pairs in the TiO₂ layer.

3

ROS generation

Reactive oxygen species form at the surface and decompose organic or biological loads.

4

Rinse response

Activated hydrophilic surface behavior improves rinse-off and reduces soiling re-adhesion.

Operating fit

Where Titan performs best.

Organic and biological fouling

Sites with organic films, biological growth, pollen or biogenic matter are well matched to Titan’s ROS-driven decomposition pathway.

Industrial atmospheric contamination

Complex atmospheric loads, including VOC-derived films and combustion residues, are amenable to oxidative breakdown where UV activation is sustained.

High-irradiance environments

High-irradiance locations provide favorable activation conditions for Titan’s photocatalytic and hydrophilic surface behavior.

UV-driven active chemistry sites

Titan is selected when an active, UV-driven route is preferable to a passive easy-clean route.
