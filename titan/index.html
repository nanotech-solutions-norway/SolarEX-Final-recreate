Mechanism

The active pathway for organic and atmospheric fouling.

Titan is selected when UV availability is sufficient and the contamination profile includes organic matter, biological fouling, atmospheric films, smog-derived residues or industrial aerosol deposition.

UV-triggered activation

TiO₂ nanoparticles absorb UV photons and generate active surface chemistry. Without sufficient UV exposure, Titan’s active route cannot deliver its intended photocatalytic function.

Reactive oxygen species

UV excitation generates electron-hole pairs and reactive oxygen species at the surface interface, including oxidative radicals that attack organic residues.

Organic decomposition

Titan targets organic films, biological loads, VOC-like residues and complex atmospheric deposits by breaking them down through photocatalytic oxidation.

Hydrophilic rinse response

Activated TiO₂ supports hydrophilic or superhydrophilic water sheeting, improving rinse-off behavior after rain or cleaning events.

Mechanism sequence

From UV exposure to rinse behavior.

1

TiO₂ nanoparticles on solar glass

A thin TiO₂ layer is deposited on cleaned photovoltaic glass, creating the active surface required for photocatalytic behavior.

2

UV photon absorption

Natural sunlight provides UV photons that activate the titanium dioxide layer and initiate electron-hole pair formation.

3

ROS generation and contaminant breakdown

Reactive oxygen species form at the surface and attack organic, biological and industrial contamination.

4

Hydrophilic rinse phase

Activated water sheeting supports rinse-off of decomposed or loosened contamination during rainfall or controlled cleaning.

Application protocol

Titan application workflow from source instructions.

Titan performance depends on a clean, dry, grease-free substrate, controlled application, mandatory hardening, final light polish and UV-supported outdoor activation.

1

Prepare the substrate

Clean the solar glass thoroughly. Remove dirt, grease, oil, silicone residues and contamination. The surface must be dry before coating.

2

Apply sparingly and uniformly

Use a lightly moistened cotton or viscose cloth and distribute the coating evenly. Avoid excessive product use and avoid uncontrolled wet application.

3

Harden before final polish

Allow the coating to harden before the final light polish. Secondary hardening/drying should be minimum 1 hour under relevant conditions.

4

Full cure and sunlight activation

Full cure is approximately 24 hours. Full outdoor effect develops over approximately 24–48 hours under sunlight depending on climate and UV availability.

Operating fit

Where Titan creates the strongest operational fit.

Organic fouling

Organic films, pollen-derived residues, VOC-like films and sticky atmospheric contaminants are aligned with Titan’s active decomposition route.

Biological contamination

Algae, biofilm, mold-related residues and other biological loads can be addressed where UV activation is sufficient.

Industrial atmosphere

Combustion residues, smog-derived deposits and mixed atmospheric loads can benefit from active TiO₂ oxidation.

High-irradiance sites

UV-sufficient environments support Titan activation and hydrophilic rinse behavior more consistently.

Pilot requirements

How to validate Titan before scale-up.

UV baseline

Document irradiance and UV availability because Titan’s mechanism depends on sunlight activation.

Contamination profile

Confirm whether the site is dominated by organic, biological or atmospheric fouling rather than mainly mineral dust.

Treated/control design

Use matched modules or strings with equivalent orientation, tilt, age and exposure profile.

Interval monitoring

Track yield, cleaning events, rainfall and visible soiling response through the review period.
