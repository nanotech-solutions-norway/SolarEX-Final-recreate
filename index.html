Why it matters

Targeted surface engineering addresses contaminant adhesion.

PV glass surfaces accumulate dust, pollen, bird lime, salt, industrial pollutants and biological residues. Contaminant adhesion affects optical interface quality and can contribute to measurable output variability. SolarEX addresses the adhesion mechanism — not only the cleaning symptom.

Optical interface protection

Cleaner glass surfaces support more stable PV operation by reducing the rate at which contaminants accumulate and bond to the glass substrate.

Reduced contaminant adhesion

Quartz reduces adhesion through hydrophobic and oleophobic SiO₂ surface behavior. Titan uses UV-activated TiO₂ photocatalysis and hydrophilic rinse behavior.

Lower cleaning burden

SolarEX supports faster, less abrasive and less water-intensive maintenance cycles by reducing contaminant adhesion and improving surface cleanability.

Mechanism-led selection

Quartz and Titan are selected through technical pathway review based on UV availability, contamination profile, geography and O&M objectives.

Platform

Two pathways. Different mechanisms.

SolarEX is not a single generic coating. It is a surface-engineering platform with two distinct product pathways, each targeting a different physical and chemical mechanism.

SolarEX Quartz — SiO₂

Ultra-thin SiO₂ nano-scale layer, 100–150 nm. Hydrophobic and oleophobic surface architecture. No UV activation required — the passive easy-clean and anti-stick mechanism operates independently of irradiance conditions.

  • Best fit: dust, pollen, bird lime, salt, grime and mineral soiling.
  • 9H pencil hardness and pH 1.5–12.5 chemical stability in source framing.
  • Manual application: approximately 15–17 mL/m²; industrial route approximately 15–20 mL/m².

SolarEX Titan — TiO₂

Alcohol-based TiO₂ nanoparticle coating. UV/natural-sunlight activated photocatalysis sustains hydrophilic film behavior. Water condenses into continuous sheets rather than droplets, supporting rinse behavior and improved optical clarity.

  • Best fit: organic, biological and atmospheric contamination where UV is sufficient.
  • Film thickness: only a few nanometers.
  • Consumption: approximately 10–25 mL/m²; wipe/polish range approximately 10–15 mL/m².

Workflow

From site assessment to evidence review.

1

Site assessment

Evaluate location, UV availability, contamination profile, cleaning burden, asset-owner objectives and pilot scope.

2

Surface preparation

Glass must be clean, dry and residue-free. Surfactants, grease, silicone residues and contamination must be removed before application.

3

Controlled application

Apply Quartz or Titan according to pathway-specific coverage, method, temperature and cure requirements.

4

Monitoring and review

Compare treated and control areas, document cleaning impact and evaluate commercial value under real operating conditions.
